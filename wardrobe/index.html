Wardrobe

The player's supervisor will get a wardrobe: hats, tops, trousers, shoes, badges and extras, each worn or taken off. Phase 1 gives the supervisor a real body. The stack of cubes is gone. The supervisor now uses the students' skinned body and skeleton, as a man or a woman (the player's choice, saved with the career). The supervisor walks with a real walk cycle, holds a hand out when acting on a student, wears the bought shirt colour, and wears the badge on the front of the chest. Phase 2 gives the wardrobe its eight slots and puts a Wardrobe in the store: a live 3D figure of your own supervisor, a Man / Woman toggle, category tabs, try-on before buying, and Buy → Wear → Take off on every item. Phase 3 puts 36 items in the game: hats, tops, trousers, shoes, pins and school crests, glasses, a lanyard and a watch, each with a price, an unlock and a shop tile, plus a hair choice for both bodies. Phase 4 measured the prices and unlocks against real careers: see Prices & balance. A polish pass adds a "keep some coins" hint to the store and fixes hair under hats, the lanyard, the shirt collar and the blazer on the woman.

In the room

Play mode on a phone (1080 × 2340) in two schools' rooms, with the Bronze badge on and the issued teal shirt. Standing beside the supervisor's desk when the room opens, and mid-walk a moment after a tap. When standing, the supervisor faces the camera, turned slightly toward the room's centre, so the face and the badge show.

The walk

20 frames a second of game time while the supervisor crosses the front of the room at full speed: first the man, then the woman. The left clip uses the game's camera angle, closer in. The right clip shows the supervisor side-on. The walk is the students' own walk clip from candidate.glb; no new clip was made. Its speed follows the supervisor's actual speed, so the planted foot stays put on the floor. The blend from standing to walking follows the speed too, so starting and stopping are gradual. A footstep sound plays each time a foot lands.

Walk speed lowered from 3.2 to 2.1 m/s (brisk walk), the user's choice. 3.2 m/s is a jog: a walk would have to play at 2.3× its speed and would look like scurrying. At 2.1 m/s the clip plays at 1.5×. The clip's planted foot travels 1.40 m/s at normal speed, measured from the clip. The balance bots now walk at 2.1 m/s too. Their twelve careful test careers were re-recorded: median final coins went from 1017 to 1010, median reputation from 70 to 68.5, and none was dismissed.

The supervisor, close up

The same figure the room uses, built on its own: as issued (teal shirt, no badge), and with the Navy Shirt and the Bronze Badge. The badge is a small disc on the chest bone, over the wearer's left chest, placed against the front of each body's shirt. It moves with the chest. Before, it was a cube that floated behind the supervisor's back.

Students are unchanged

The students' body file, parts file and prefab are untouched. The supervisor uses them read-only and has its own material. In the Maple room the same class is seated before and after the change: the students are identical, and only the supervisor differs. In the National room the class draw depends on the frame count, so different people are seated, but they are drawn the same way. A test builds both supervisors and checks that every student renderer's colour and material is unchanged.

Phase 2: the Wardrobe

The store's "Appearance" section is now the Wardrobe. Play mode at a phone's portrait size (1080 × 2340), dark theme, with the Navy Shirt and the Bronze Badge owned, the badge worn, and 160 coins.

On the phone

The Wardrobe works on the phone. Galaxy A15 (SM-S156V, Android 16), development build of 9b848d2: phases 2 and 3 together. The render errors are gone: logcat shows 0 "samples were requested" and 0 "Not inside a Renderpass" lines over about 13 minutes in the Wardrobe, and 0 again after leaving the store and playing into an exam. The app logged no Unity errors or exceptions, and the development console never opened.

What was wrong on the first phone build (6ac9729). The preview's texture asked for 2× anti-aliasing while the phone's render pipeline has it off. Every pass of the preview failed, twice a frame, until the app closed. Without the depth test the skin and trousers drew over the shirt, and the picture stopped updating, so try-ons and turns never reached the screen. The texture is now single-sampled, and the camera stops the moment you leave the store.

Tap freezes, fixed in place. On 9b848d2 every Wardrobe tap redrew the whole store, all ~37 tiles, and a body or hair change also saved a long career at once (one serialization and two parses of a 286 k-character file). Redressing the figure was never the cost: about 0.1 ms. Now a tap changes only what it touches: the figure is redressed in place, a choice moves without rebuilding its row, a try-on only moves a tile's outline, a tab hides the other tabs' tiles out of view without switching them off, and the rows and tiles draw on their own canvases. An outfit, body or hair change is saved when you leave the store. Only buying redraws the page.

Eight slots

Hat, top, trousers, shoes, badge, glasses, neck (lanyard or tie) and wrist (watch). One item each, owned items only. Every slot can be emptied except the top: taking a top off puts the issued shirt back on. Saves move from version 3 to 4; the old "shirt" becomes the top, so nothing worn or owned is lost.

The seams on the woman's tops: fixed

Before · shipped Top_LongSleeve_F

Thin light slits at the shoulder and at the sides of the waist.

After · in the game now

No slits, same shape and weights.

Cause. To shape her bust, build_female.py subdivides the chest of the male body once. The faces inside that patch gain a vertex in the middle of each edge; the face across the patch's border keeps its plain edge. The body is flat-shaded, so the tops are cut from it and welded by position, and a vertex in the middle of an edge has nothing to weld to. The gap is invisible until the vertex moves (the bust) and the shell is pushed out along its normals. Then it opens into a slit. There are exactly 12 of these on each female top (short-sleeve, hoodie, long-sleeve) and none on the male tops. Fix. When the shell is cut, each such edge is split at the middle vertex and welded (fix_tjunctions, a few lines in build_parts.shell). It also fixes the female students, who wear the same parts. It is in the game: 12 T-junctions closed on each of her three tops (a test checks the shipped meshes have none).

Polish — in the game (6b7f9b2)

Keep some coins, and the clothes polished

A calm hint in the store when a purchase would leave too little for a surprise bill, and five fixes to the clothes: hair under hats, the lanyard, the shirt collar, the blazer on the woman, and a check of the female students. Before and after pairs are Blender renders with the same camera and light (white items, as in the phase 3 checks); the rest is the game.

Keep some coins

Why

The balance study (Prices & balance) found that players who spend every coin cannot pay a car repair or medicine at the door when ill, and their careers fall behind. Keeping about 100¢ back removes the effect.

The reserve

One car repair (80¢) plus one door price of medicine (half the salary) at your current school: 105¢ at Maple, more at better-paid schools. A pill already at home covers the ill morning, so then only the repair counts. One rule in Core (CoinReserve), easy to tune.

The hint

Under the price of any tile that would leave you below it: Leaves you 60 ¢, one line in the warning colour, no icon, no motion. The balance card says why, once: Keep some coins for emergencies. About 105 ¢ covers surprises like a car repair or medicine at the door. Tools, pills and clothes alike.

Never in the way

The Buy button is unchanged and one tap still buys. Nothing shows above the reserve, or on a tile you cannot buy anyway. The line fits at the largest text size.

Hair under hats

Before, the bob and the curly hair came out through the side and crown of every hat: the hats were fitted round the short hairs only. Now each hairstyle has an under-a-hat version: whatever a hat covers is drawn just inside the tightest of the five hats, and the hair below the brim is left as it is. Every style, every hat, both bodies: vertices outside a hat went from 1,039 (bob), 814 (curly), 274 (long), 144 (short), 115 (tied-back) and 75 (cropped) to 0. The bun is still let down into the ponytail under a hat.

The lanyard

Before, one lanyard was placed 3 cm off the skin to clear a blazer's lapels, so on a T-shirt the card floated. Now there is one per top and body (16): every point is laid on that top's own front, just clear of it, and bridges a collar, a tie or a lapel. Checked side-on through the walk.

The shirt collar

The shirt-and-tie collar points dropped 6 cm in two straight steps and read as blocks. They now ease down over seven rows of a finer band and lean in toward the knot. Still flat-shaded and low-poly.

The blazer on the woman

The lapels creased into pleats over her bust: they were a fan of thin triangles, each lifted along its own facet's slope. They are now rows of quads that drape: lifted along a softened normal and relaxed toward their neighbours, never closer to the cloth than half their lift. The cardigan's front bands, which had the same problem, are built the same way. The man's blazer is cleaner too.

The female students

The seam fix (above) re-cut the female students' tops. They look right in the classroom: no slits, same shapes and colours. This pass does not change the students' parts at all.

Other spending

Tools: 430¢ once

Phone Detector 250, Replay 180. A permanent unlock, bought by exam 6–11 in every style. After that nothing else in the store costs more than a pill, except clothes.

Pills: 15–57¢

The door price is half the salary; a stocked pill is 40% off it (15 at Maple, 57 at National). Keeping one in stock costs a careful career about 190¢ over 60 exams, and 45–100¢ for the other styles. It never competes with clothes.

No coffee

The store sells only tools, medicine, clothes and real-money packs. Coffee is just a line in an event text. Living costs, car repairs and fines are charged; they are not purchases.

The wardrobe is the only sink

After the tools, a careful career has about 7,000¢ and nothing else to spend it on. The wardrobe takes 8,490¢, so it is the long-term goal: a careful career finishes it 15 exams after the second distinction (exam ~46).

Two kinds of shopper. A saver buys the tools, then lets coins pile up: the "basic outfit" and "if saving" columns show the first exam its balance covers the goal (basic outfit = beanie, T-shirt, jeans, sneakers). A wanting shopper buys the tools, keeps one pill, then picks a random item on sale and saves for it. The other columns use this shopper. Items owned include reward drops.

  • No dead stretch for a good career. The prices climb in small steps (90, 100, 120, 140, 160 …), so the next item is never far off. The longest wait is set by income: about 10 exams careful, 15–23 for a slow band-1 career. That is a goal, not a dead end.
  • Nothing comes too early. At band 3 a careful player buys about one item every 2 exams (1.96). They still finish only at exam ~62, because there are 38 items.
  • The band-3 items are a short goal. Unlocked at exam 13, a saver can afford all three by exam 24: about 3.5 band-3 exams each. A 140¢ item costs a band-1 player 4–7 exams.
  • Spending every coin hurts middling careers. When a good career spends down to zero, band-3 reach drops from 44% to 31%. An average career reaches band 2 in 8 of 48 instead of 19 of 48. The reason is that a broke player can't pay for a car repair or medicine, so they push through and have worse exams. Keeping 100¢ back removes the effect completely (44%, 19/48), in all three seed blocks. This applies to any spending, not just these prices.

The reward pool

Navy (100), Charcoal (140), Bronze (120) and Silver (250) are still the only items drawn. A careful career gets one by exam 20 and has all four by exam ~40 (44 of 48 careers). Every other style usually ends with one (Navy). Once all four are owned, a cosmetic draw pays coins instead. Silver drops only at band 3 but is sold from band 2, so a drop never gets ahead of the shop. Keep it as it is. Adding the new items would change every balance fingerprint and could hand out a crest before its school.

Recommendation and decision

Recommendations 1 and 2 are in the game; 3 and 4 needed no change.

  1. Lock the ten 190–260¢ items to band 2 (Supervisor). Prices unchanged. Band 1 then sells only items up to 160¢ (16 items and 3 crests, 2,630¢ in all). The tools (180, 250) are the only big buys while you need them most. No T-shirt-versus-detector choice at the same price, and a second store opening at the first promotion. How it was measured: Core and Balance from 7d8747d, compiled with Unity's bundled Roslyn and run on its .NET 6 runtime, outside Unity. Careers go through CareerRunner with EventPolicy, onboarding on and phone rooms. Proposed prices were tried by changing the catalogue in the probe's memory only. Bots never use tools, so careers that own them may do slightly better than measured. The "good" and "average" bots are FalliblePolicy with its error rates cut to ¼ and ½. The plain Fallible bot is dismissed in most careers (as M124 found), so it stands for a sloppy player.

How it works

One builder

SupervisorFigure.Build(prefab, appearance, parent) makes a dressed figure anywhere, from a SupervisorAppearance (body, hair and every wardrobe slot). The room and the store's live preview both use it.

Man or woman

Saved in the career as SupervisorBody, Male by default. Older saves load as Male. It is only a look: the simulation never reads it. The Wardrobe's toggle changes it at any time, free.

Three clips, one mixer

Standing, Walking and PresentDocument (the reach), from the students' file. They are mixed by speed on a hand-driven graph, with no per-frame allocation.

Mobile cost

One skinned body, a skinned part per garment worn (at most about seven), a small pin and one material.

Known limits

  • Buying still redraws the whole store. A purchase moves the balance and with it what every card can afford, so the page is rebuilt: about 50 ms in the editor; not yet timed on the phone. Every other Wardrobe tap updates in place.
  • A changed outfit is saved when you leave the store. While the Wardrobe is open, a change of clothes, body or hair waits and is written when the store closes or the app is put away, so the save's cost lands on that screen change instead of on each tap.
  • Older careers' school history is inferred. A career saved before the school record counts the main ladder below its schools. A branch school it really worked at (Brightpath) stays locked unless the save shows it.
  • One colour per item. Each item has its own fixed colours; there are no colour variants to choose.
  • Prices and unlocks. The two approved changes are in (Prices & balance); the rest are the phase 3 values.
