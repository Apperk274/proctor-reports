Career Balance

How fast a career climbs from Maple to the National Examination Centre, how easily it moves between schools, what it earns and when the store is bought. Today's game is measured against the targets, and three tuning packages are proposed. The packages run in the probes only: nothing in the game has changed yet.

Each figure comes from 36 bot careers × 100 exams per player type, on two seed blocks. Careers start at Maple with the tutorial on, in phone rooms, with a tablet arm for the key numbers. The player types are a careful player, a careful player who also inspects, a beginner careful by exam 10, a beginner careful by exam 20, an error-prone player, and a careful player who takes the money.

Why the climb is fast and moves are easy

  • Standing grows by about 1.8 an exam. A careful exam is Good 85–100% of the time in the first schools, and every Good exam earns +2. Almost all of a careful player's standing comes from this. Poor exams are rare for careful play: it falls short with Mixed results, which cost nothing.
  • The offer is the best school the standing has opened, with only a 2-exam wait between offers. National's 85 is the only real gate. Standing then reaches its cap of 100 by about exam 40–60, so the late career has nothing at stake.
  • The first promotion comes inside the tutorial. A career starts at 40, Riverside asks 44, and the first two lessons are scripted to go well. Beginners are promoted at exam 2–3 and demoted at about exam 11, once the door opens and their mistakes count. That is 18 of beginner-slow's 22 demotions in one seed block.
  • Floors sit only 10 below each requirement, and one wrongful-removal review costs 10, so one bad exam can demote.
  • Dismissal is not the problem for learners. No beginner is dismissed. The error-prone player is dismissed in 34–35 of 36 careers, at about exam 38, mainly for wrongly turning people away at the door. Money is never the cause: at most 3 in 36 careers are ever in debt.

  • 40 coin items, 9820 coins in all today. There are 2 tools (Phone Detector 400; Replay 180, given free in the tutorial), 30 clothes and pins, and 8 school crests. Pills cost 30% of the salary.
  • Any single item is affordable early. Each first-school item can be bought on its own by exam 2–6, band-2 items by 7–11 and the 750 band-3 items by 15–19. The store is not out of reach; the problem is that the top band pays for all of it at once.
  • The tools' value could not be measured. No bot uses a tool. The sound-hint study found that the Phone Detector does not change how many cheaters are caught, and that its two uses run out in about 3% of exams.

The first ten exams

  • A careful player sees about 20 cards in 10 exams today, including 3.2 job offers while the tutorial is still teaching. With any package that is about 15–16 cards and a single offer, after exam 6–8.
  • A slow learner's standing drifts down, from 42 to 37 by exam 10. Each exam it swings 2.7 points on average, with 1.8 drops of 5 or more per career, and 9% of exams are Poor. The packages do not change this, because it is the player's mistakes. What changes is that no promotion comes early, so there is none to lose.
  • At most one final warning comes before exam 10 in any learner arm, as M132 found.

  • Today there are two gaps. Between Kingsley (exam 10–13) and National there is nothing new but saving, because every item is already unlocked. After exam about 65–74 there is nothing new at all, and standing sits at 100.
  • Each package adds one small reason to play that needs no new screen. Each reuses the existing reward card:
    • Gentle: 100 coins on the first exam at each new school.
    • Target: a school's crest is given after 10 exams worked there.
    • Long: a long-service bonus of 150 coins at career exams 25, 50, 75 and 100.
  • None of them fills exams 70–100. That needs a late goal or a late thing to spend on, such as a National-only collection or a third distinction. Both need new UI.

The three packages

All three share one shape:

  • A minimum stay at each school before any job offer. A move to an equally rated school that pays more does not restart the count.
  • One rung at a time: the next rating step among schools that pay more.
  • A grace period in which a new posting cannot be lost.

Careful players move on that clock. Learners are held back by the standing each school asks.

Recommendation: Target

  • It hits the targets. Careful play reaches the top at 58, and learners arrive visibly later (65–77 on phone). The tutorial promotion is gone, slow learners stop bouncing, and standing still matters at the top. The store is finished about 9 exams after the top, and money moves from the top of the career towards the bottom.
  • Trade-offs:
    • About 39% fewer coins by exam 100, with prices cut to match.
    • Careful pace is a clock, so everyone who plays well arrives at 58.
    • On tablet, slow learners take about 82–88 exams.
    • 2 in 36 careful-but-not-inspecting careers do not reach National by exam 100 (up to 6 on tablet): the standing asked at the top binds for weak top-school play.
  • The store prices are proposals for the other session's catalogue.
    • T-shirts 90 → 50, the Cap and Glasses 140 → 80, first-school crests 150 → 90.
    • Phone Detector 400 → 240.
    • Band-2 clothes 190–260 → 100–140, the City High crest 300 → 160, top-band crests 500 → 300.
    • Blazer, Gold Pin and Graduation Cap 750 → 450.
    • The total falls from 9820 to 5700.
  • Needs UI from the other session. The clock must be visible: "N more exams here before the next offer" on the Career tab and the hub. The three reward ideas reuse the existing reward card.
  • Needs core work in a phase 2. A minimum-stay rule per school, the one-rung rule in the promotion choice, and the new-posting grace. Everything else is an existing value.

Caveats

  • Bots are not players. They never use tools or shop, and the beginner's learning curve is a straight line from error-prone to careful.
  • The packages are emulated in the probes by opening and closing schools' requirements during play. A real implementation needs its own tests.
  • The two seed blocks agree within a few exams on every careful pace figure and within about 5% on coins.
  • The figures come from the editor's batchmode run. A faster console build of the same code gives medians within a few exams, but not identical careers, because floating point differs between runtimes.
