Student Characters

The classroom is now filled with real students instead of block figures. Each student is one shared body, male or female, dressed from who they are and what they wore today: hair, skin, eyes, glasses and a top. They write, read, glance, whisper, reach, raise a hand, hold things up, think, stretch and walk, with clips fitted to the game's own desk and chair. About one in ten is left-handed and does everything the other way round. Every image and video below is from this project.

How it is built

No decimation

Every low part is built by script on a fixed topology and fitted to the kit by rays: tops from one template (16-column torso, yoke, 8-sided sleeves), trousers from a pelvis that splits into two legs, mitten hands, shoes, glasses and beards. The kit gives shape and skin weights, not triangles.

Child heads

The kit head reshaped, not new topology: one smooth field over face, eyes and hair compresses the lower face to 80 % and lowers the eyes in the head, grows the cranium 4.5 % and the eyes 32 %, shrinks the nose to 45 %, narrows the jaw, fills the cheeks and pulls the chin back, then smooths the jaw and cheekbones. The neck bone shrinks to 0.6 and the head is 0.93 of an adult's on a 1.33 m body. Ratios from MakeHuman children measured in MPFB; young teens get about half of it.

Hair

A hull of each kit style from rays out of the skull, hairlines placed where the kit's hair actually ends, bald patches inside the hair filled. 440–580 triangles instead of 1,100–1,500, without the torn shapes blind decimation gave.

One material

Every face's UVs point at its role's cell in a 16 × 16 palette. The game builds a palette per student from their traits and sets it in a property block on one URP Lit material, instead of tinting each role submesh as today.

Proctor fixes kept

Neck and sleeve closures, sleeve settling over the check poses, the proctor's build field for slim and heavy adults, glasses arms laid outside the skin, stable caps, two-sided collars with a real back side.

Bands

One deformation field per band for the head, then the band's proportions (height, head, neck, legs, hands, slimness) baked into its own rest pose; adults keep slim and heavy blend shapes.

Rough edges

  • Short sleeves fail the poke check in hard poses. In WritingLoop and ReachBag the upper arm shows under the lifted sleeve opening: 1.0–1.8 mm for children and teens, 4–14 mm for heavy-build adults. It is the arm visible under the opening, not fabric pierced (the proctor's report lists the same), and settling the fabric cannot remove it. The check stays failing so it is visible.
  • Hair is smoother than the kit's. The hulls keep each style's silhouette and hairline at 460–580 triangles, but lose the kit's separate locks; spiky reads less spiky and some fringes are blocky at the door.
  • The beard is a front patch. At three-quarter view skin shows between the beard and the hair along the jaw.
  • Mitten hands are simple. A flat paddle with a thumb, fine from the game camera, plain at the door.
  • Glasses are octagonal and the arms stand off wide child heads. Every check passes, but the frame shape is basic.
  • Skirts stretch between the legs when walking (edges up to 6–9 times their length in the walk). Standing and seated are fine; a skirt solution belongs with animation in phase 2.
  • Children's feet only just reach the floor from the 0.475 m seat rather than dangling; seated renders place the hands on the desk by IK, which phase 2 replaces with real desk-contact clips.
  • Each band has its own rig with different bone lengths; clips must play as rotations (with the hips height scaled), which is phase 2's job.
  • Girls and boys in the child band differ mainly by hair and the skirt, which is true to the age but makes the gender read rely on hair.

The students in the classroom

The game spawns the skinned students in its own Classroom scene. What a student does is still decided only by what the player could see; the body shows it with a clip. These frames are from Play mode.

Who a student is

A student's look comes in two parts. The person in the seat is always the person the admission card describes.

Who they are

Gender, skin, eyes, hair and glasses. The same in every exam, and checked against the admission photo. Skin and eyes on the photo always match the holder. Hair and glasses on the photo can be out of date (a haircut since), so a different face settles it and a different haircut is only a question.

What they wore today

The top's style and colour. New for each exam, never on the photo, never compared.

Which hand they use

About one in ten students is left-handed, for life. It is never on the admission document and changes nothing in play. Left-handers play mirrored clips and hold things in the left hand.

Women and men, one skeleton

Gender picks the body, the first name (eight male and eight female first names) and the hairstyles: short, cropped and curly for men; wavy, long and tied-back for women. The female body is the male one reshaped (chest, waist, hips, shoulders, neck, jaw) on the same joints, so every animation, hairstyle and pair of glasses works on both. Both are the same height, so both fit the chair and desk.

Six hairstyles

Five from Meshy. Tied-back is the cropped cap with a ponytail built in Blender. Hair is cut clear of the eyes, because eye colour is checked at the door.

Left-handed students

Left-handers write, erase, hold things, check a watch, raise a hand, stretch, think, stand, walk and hold out a document on the other side. Gestures aimed at a place (a neighbour, a bag, the clock) still turn toward that place, whichever hand the student uses.

Wardrobe: three tops on both bodies

Short-sleeve t-shirt, long-sleeve t-shirt and a hoodie with a real hood lying on the back, a rolled rim, a kangaroo pocket and drawstrings. Each top is cut from its body's own torso and stands off the skin by 1.2, 1.8 or 2.6 cm. Front, side, back and from above (roughly the supervisor's angle), in the rest pose. The colours here are two palette entries; in the game the colour changes with what the student wore that day.

How it's built

One body built by a Blender script and rigged by Meshy's auto-rig, in a male and a female shape on the same skeleton, with hair, glasses and tops as swappable parts on that skeleton, tinted per student. The motions are Meshy text-to-motion clips seated on the game's chair and pinned to the real desk in Blender, plus clips made by hand there: 44 in all, including the mirrors for left-handers and the hand-made whisperer and listener. The game drives the body through the existing pose contract: each pose maps to a clip, changes fade smoothly, held gestures wind down with their own motion, and the game's own clock drives it all, so it stays deterministic and testable.

Known limitations

  • Neighbours sit 3 m apart, so a whispering pair lean and turn to each other but stay well apart; from the overview camera the listener's lean is small.
  • The clock is on the back wall, behind the students. Checking the clock is aimed at its side but reads as a look at the front wall. Parked until the room's layout is revisited.
  • Innocent gestures can outlast their signal. A clock check, a watch check or a stretch plays to its natural end before the student settles back to writing, so the body can lag the simulation by up to about 4 s. Any cue interrupts at once.
  • Thinking hard can read as head-in-hands from the front while the dominant hand taps the temple (about 1.5 s of each 6 s loop).
  • The propping elbow in thinking hard floats in front of the body: the rig's arm can't reach the desk from an upright seat.
  • Short and cropped hair look alike. One may want regenerating.
  • A held phone is small from the overview camera, about 10 px across at 1600×900. The coral case and lit blue screen make it stand out, but less than when it was tilted toward the camera.
  • A held paper reads greyer than the sheet on the desk, because it faces the holder, away from the light.
  • Mirrors are within 1–2 cm of a perfect reflection, because the rig is slightly lopsided: one forearm is 1.4 cm shorter than the other.
  • A writing student sits back for a moment before reaching out, because the reach starts upright with the hands on the thighs.
  • Holding an item and thinking hard loop inside the gesture, so getting into and out of them is covered by the fade alone.
