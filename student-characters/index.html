Student Characters

Hands, tops, chests, upright child writing and the new glance: in review · PR #18, not merged yet

The game's students are built from the proctor's kit in four age bands, child to adult, with one body per band and sex, dressed from who they are and what they wore today: hair, skin, eyes, glasses, facial hair and clothes. They write, read, glance, whisper, reach, raise a hand, hold things up, think, stretch and walk, with clips baked per band onto the game's own desk and chair. About one in ten is left-handed and does everything the other way round. They replaced an earlier set of students, which the second half of this page keeps for the record. Every image and video below is from this project.

Hands, tops and chests

Renders from the exported meshes on the baked clips: the students as they were merged (before) beside the current ones (now).

The hand used to be a thumb and a flat paddle on a pinched wrist. It is now a mitten with a palm: it sits on the kit hand's real knuckles, with a flattened palm and the thumb rooted on the palm side, at 0.8 of the kit hand's size. The fingers stay one block; there are still no finger bones.

Tops no longer bulge. A missed shoulder column used to copy the shoulder tip, which stood a fin of 37–52 mm off the tee's back; it now sits halfway between the armpit and the neckline, and the fin is gone. Tops are fitted to the garment's main piece only, so collars, ties, hoods and drawstrings no longer push the chest or back out: polo and shirt fronts are 1–2 cm flatter.

Every women's top now has a bust with an underside, placed from the kit woman's own top: a full one for older teens and adults, a small one for young teens and none for child girls, who stay flat. Long sleeves, sweaters and cardigans get the same apex and underside as the tee, and the bust no longer pushes the waist out. Women's tops are 32 triangles heavier for the row under the bust.

How it is built

No decimation

Every low part is built by script on a fixed topology and fitted to the kit by rays: tops from one template (16-column torso, yoke, 8-sided sleeves), trousers from a pelvis that splits into two legs, hands, shoes, glasses and beards. The kit gives shape and skin weights, not triangles.

Hands

A mitten laid on the kit hand's real knuckles: a flattened palm ring, then knuckle and finger rings sized by rays against the kit hand, with the thumb rooted on the palm side. The whole hand is 0.8 of the kit's size. It hangs from the wrist bone; the rig has no finger bones.

Child heads

The kit head reshaped, not new topology. Child faces are first blended 55 % towards the Round face; then one smooth field over face, eyes and hair compresses the lower face to 80 % (the eyes sit lower in the head), grows the cranium 4.5 % and the eyes 32 %, shrinks the nose to 45 %, widens the jaw near the chin, fills the cheeks and pulls the chin back, and smoothing softens the jaw and cheekbones. Neck bone 0.6, head 0.93 of an adult's on a 1.33 m body. Ratios from MakeHuman children measured in MPFB.

Hair

The kit's own locks: every face no camera can see (240 directions, the faces as occluders) is removed, coplanar faces are merged only as far as the budget needs, and lock insides that can show get a back side. The scalp under every style is hair-coloured. After each band's head is reshaped, skull skin that would reach a lock is pulled under it, and any skin still showing inside the hair from above, the sides, behind or the classroom camera is covered by a hair-coloured patch; the build repeats the hair-cover check's own test until it finds nothing.

Clothes

Tops are fitted to the kit garment's main piece only, so its collar, tie, hood or drawstrings do not push the chest or back out. Women's tops take their front rows from the kit woman's own top, with a row under the bust, baked per band (none for children). Torso and sleeve radii are smoothed around and along the body so the planes read as deliberate rather than as the kit garment's folds; polos and shirts have a raised placket; short-sleeve hems are weighted like the arm under them, which ended the arm showing under the sleeve; shoes have a flat sole slab, a heel and a lower toe box.

One material

Every face's UVs point at its role's cell in a 16 × 16 palette. The game builds a palette per student from their traits and sets it in a property block on one URP Lit material, instead of tinting each role submesh as the previous students did.

Proctor fixes kept

Neck and sleeve closures, sleeve settling over the check poses, the proctor's build field for slim and heavy adults, glasses arms laid outside the skin, stable caps, collars with a real back side, sleeve hems weighted like the arm under them.

Feet on the floor

The kit foot bone lies flat, but the game's clips were authored on a skeleton whose foot bone points 50° down at rest. Copying that direction tipped every student onto their toes, so the lowest point was a toe and the soles hung off the floor. The retarget now carries the clip's foot rotation relative to its own rest pose, and the foot hangs from the shin at the ankle, so a standing student stands on flat soles straight from the clip. Seated, teens and adults plant both feet flat; children's feet hang just above the floor, relaxed.

Faces: brows, glasses, nose

The kit woman's face folds back under itself across the forehead; that hidden inner layer is removed, since its edges showed through as frown-like creases once the head was reshaped. The child eye growth spreads outwards from each eye's inner corner and fades out gently, so the forehead is moved rather than squeezed. Between the eyes the nose bridge is pushed back behind the eye line for children and young teens, so it sits low and soft with one small nose below. Brows keep a gap at the middle, the glasses lens top stays under the brows and the bridge rests on the nose. Scalp that would show below a fringe at the door stays skin-coloured.

Bands

One deformation field per band for the head, then the band's proportions (height, head, neck, legs, hands, slimness) baked into its own rest pose; adults keep slim and heavy blend shapes.

Rough edges

  • Hair is the kit's, hairline gaps included. Where a lock's edge lies on the skin at the temples (pixie, tied back, long) a thin skin-coloured crack can show between locks at three-quarter view; nothing pokes through or above any style (the hair-cover check), and the forehead may show between fringe strands by design.
  • The budget is tight for men: up to 2,990 triangles (an adult man with spiky hair, a polo, glasses and a moustache); women reach 2,872.
  • The beard is a front patch. At three-quarter view skin shows between the beard and the hair along the jaw.
  • Glasses are octagonal and the arms stand off wide child heads. Every glasses check passes, but the frame shape is basic.
  • Skirts stretch between the legs when walking (edges up to 6–9 times their length).

Each student is one skinned mesh merged from their parts, on one URP Lit material, with the colours in a per-student 16 × 16 palette set through a property block. Counted in the five rooms above: every student's body is one renderer with one material, in the colour pass and in the shadows; a prop in the hand (one tissue at Eastgate) adds its own.

The paper placed per band

Each exam paper moves towards its writer by band: 8 cm for children, 11 cm for young teens, 9 cm for older teens, 8 cm for adults (option A in desk distance). The room, desks and chairs stay where they were. In the writers measured in the rooms above (45 across five rooms and four runs), the writing hand is on the sheet.

Photo and door framing per body

The ID photo and the door close-up are framed on the student's own face rather than on the tallest hair: each body's chin and bare skull top, measured over its four faces, are zoomed to fill the frame as the previous students' head did, with the head set a little low to leave room for ordinary hairstyles. Children, teens and adults fill both frames the same way.

Clips per band

Every prefab plays its own band's baked clips under the names the game already uses. The prefab builder measures each body's hold, release and settle times from those clips; letting go of a held gesture fades over 1.0–1.6 s.

What the bake does

Rotations, per band

Each source frame is retargeted by rotation with whole-frame alignment onto the band's own skeleton, feet relative to the clip's own rest. Only the hips (Body) carry a translation, scaled to the band.

Seat

Children sit 16.5 cm forward of the seat point, adults 10 cm, so they can reach the desk. The trouser mesh is measured every second frame and the body raised or lowered until it rests on the seat; a thigh may press at most about 2 cm into the seat's front edge.

Hands

The writing hand and the hand holding the sheet are on the paper. Resting hands go on the desk where the source had them near it, within 85 % of the band's arm; where the desk is out of that reach for most of a clip they rest on the thigh instead. Each hand's height comes from its own mesh; the elbow swings to the lowest position that keeps the forearm out of the desk, and hands are kept out of the thighs.

Lean

Where a target is beyond about 90 % of the arm, the spine leans forward (sideways to the bag) within a per-band limit. The head turns back what it should not follow, so glances, clock checks and whispers look where the source looks.

Children's writing

Writing and WritingLoop for children bake from upright source clips. The child sits 3.5 cm further forward, leans up to 36° from the hips with the back straight, the head bowed and the forearms forward to the paper's near edge; a last pass keeps the arms out of the desk.

Letting go of a held clip

The game lets go of ReachBag or HandRaised by playing its wind-down while it fades into the next pose, blending each bone the shortest way round. Where an upper arm would pass the opposite of its writing rotation in that fade, the bake replaces that stretch of the arm with a smooth turn between clear frames, so the blend cannot flip it round the other way.

Feet

Young teens, older teens and adults plant both feet flat at one spot per rig, the same in every seated clip, so cross-fades do not slide them; IdleCalm no longer lifts a heel. Children's legs hang relaxed, the foot 14° toe-down; the soles hang 10–20 cm above the floor (a little more than the 10–15 cm aimed for).

Standing and walking

Standing and PresentDocument plant both feet flat for the whole clip. The walk keeps its stride, and the lowest shoe vertex is put on the floor every frame.

Previous students: who a student was

A previous student's look came in two parts. The person in the seat was always the person the admission card described.

Who they are

Gender, skin, eyes, hair and glasses. The same in every exam, and checked against the admission photo. Skin and eyes on the photo always match the holder. Hair and glasses on the photo can be out of date (a haircut since), so a different face settles it and a different haircut is only a question.

What they wore today

The top's style and colour. New for each exam, never on the photo, never compared.

Which hand they use

About one in ten students is left-handed, for life. It is never on the admission document and changes nothing in play. Left-handers play mirrored clips and hold things in the left hand.

Women and men, one skeleton

Gender picked the body, the first name (eight male and eight female first names) and the hairstyles: short, cropped and curly for men; wavy, long and tied-back for women. The female body was the male one reshaped (chest, waist, hips, shoulders, neck, jaw) on the same joints, so every animation, hairstyle and pair of glasses worked on both. Both were the same height, so both fitted the chair and desk.

Six hairstyles

Five from Meshy. Tied-back was the cropped cap with a ponytail built in Blender. Hair was cut clear of the eyes, because eye colour is checked at the door.

Left-handed students

Left-handers wrote, erased, held things, checked a watch, raised a hand, stretched, thought, stood, walked and held out a document on the other side. Gestures aimed at a place (a neighbour, a bag, the clock) still turned toward that place, whichever hand the student used.

How the previous students were built

One body built by a Blender script and rigged by Meshy's auto-rig, in a male and a female shape on the same skeleton, with hair, glasses and tops as swappable parts on that skeleton, tinted per student. The motions were Meshy text-to-motion clips seated on the game's chair and pinned to the real desk in Blender, plus clips made by hand there: 44 in all, including the mirrors for left-handers and the hand-made whisperer and listener. The game drove the body through the existing pose contract: each pose maps to a clip, changes fade smoothly, held gestures wind down with their own motion, and the game's own clock drives it all, so it stays deterministic and testable. The current students use the same contract.

Known limitations of the previous students

As they stood when the age-banded students replaced them.

  • Neighbours sit 3 m apart, so a whispering pair lean and turn to each other but stay well apart; from the overview camera the listener's lean is small.
  • The clock is on the back wall, behind the students. Checking the clock is aimed at its side but reads as a look at the front wall. Parked until the room's layout is revisited.
  • Innocent gestures can outlast their signal. A clock check, a watch check or a stretch plays to its natural end before the student settles back to writing, so the body can lag the simulation by up to about 4 s. Any cue interrupts at once.
  • Thinking hard can read as head-in-hands from the front while the dominant hand taps the temple (about 1.5 s of each 6 s loop).
  • The propping elbow in thinking hard floats in front of the body: the rig's arm can't reach the desk from an upright seat.
  • Short and cropped hair look alike. One may want regenerating.
  • A held phone is small from the overview camera, about 10 px across at 1600×900. The coral case and lit blue screen make it stand out, but less than when it was tilted toward the camera.
  • A held paper reads greyer than the sheet on the desk, because it faces the holder, away from the light.
  • Mirrors are within 1–2 cm of a perfect reflection, because the rig is slightly lopsided: one forearm is 1.4 cm shorter than the other.
  • A writing student sits back for a moment before reaching out, because the reach starts upright with the hands on the thighs.
  • Holding an item and thinking hard loop inside the gesture, so getting into and out of them is covered by the fade alone.
