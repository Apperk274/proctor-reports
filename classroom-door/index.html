Exam Supervisor Simulator · during the exam

Classroom Door

When the supervisor removes a student during the exam, the student stands up and walks out. The owner saw them walk inside the back wall to get to the door. Now they walk the aisle and the passage behind the back row, go through the door, which opens for them, and are gone. The door closes behind them. Captured in Play mode on the room camera at 1080×2340 portrait with six students. The rest of the screen is hidden to show the room.

Verdict

Fixed. From every seat, with or without a bag, the body stays out of the walls, desks, chairs, bags and other students, and leaves only through the doorway. Removal itself is unchanged: the student is out the moment the supervisor decides, and cannot be tapped while they walk.

The way out from every seat

  • Planned for the body. The walking clip draws the body 0.25 m ahead of the student's root. The route is the path of the body, and the root follows 0.25 m behind it, turning round behind it at corners. The route starts where the student stands up, 0.25 m in front of the chair.
  • The admission lanes. It reuses the line at the door's geometry. Up the left side of the centre aisle (x = −1.35, the side people already leave by), along the passage behind the back row (z = 9.1), through the doorway at x = −3.2 and into the corridor.
  • Round the bag. If the student's bag is on the aisle side, they step out on the other side, walk up past their chair and come back to the aisle behind it. From the back row, the space behind the chair is the passage, so they go straight to the door.
  • Out of sight first. The body is switched off only once all of it is behind the wall, 0.25 m into the corridor. Until then it is visible in the doorway, and the dark doorway hides it as it goes through.
  • One at a time. If a second student is removed while someone is still walking out, they stand at their desk until the first is gone, then set off. Nobody walks into anybody.

The door

  • Held open, not set open. The door is open while anyone holds it open and closed when nobody does. The admission holds it during admission. The walk-out holds it from the moment of removal until the student is gone. Neither can close it on the other.
  • Open in time. Standing up takes 0.6 s and the shortest walk to the doorway is about 2.7 m, so the door (a 0.7 s swing) is always fully open before anyone reaches it.
  • The bell. If the exam ends while someone is walking out, they are out at once, as before, and the door is let go.
  • Bathroom trips do not use the door. A student who goes out to the bathroom still disappears where they stand, by design: being away is about presence, not location. Only a removal goes through the door.
  • Unchanged. The student is marked removed the moment the supervisor decides, cannot be tapped while walking out, and is never walked out again after loading a save. With Reduce motion on there is no walk and the door stays shut.

Tests

  • From every seat, every bag case. Every body position on the way out stays 0.2 m clear of every desk, every other chair and its sitter, every bag, the teacher's desk, the side and front walls and the open door leaf. It meets the back wall only at the doorway's centre line and ends with the whole body behind the wall.
  • The live walk. Driving the real walk-out tick by tick from every seat, the student's root + 0.25 m stays on that route and clear of the same things.
  • The door. Held open from removal until the student is gone, then let go; never closed on the admission; let go at the bell. A second removal waits for the first.
  • In the real room. Booting the classroom and removing students one after another, every frame: the walking student's hips never cross the wall except in the doorway, never enter a desk or a bag, and never come within 0.45 m of another student. The door is fully open whenever they are near it, the body is switched off only behind the wall, and the door is fully closed 1.5 s after they are gone.
