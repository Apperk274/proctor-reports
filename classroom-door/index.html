Classroom Door and Walking Paths

The owner saw students walk into walls and the door when they left the class. Measured across all eight school rooms, on a phone and a tablet, from every seat: almost every walk a student took went into something. Now each walk follows the room's clear floor, goes through the middle of a real doorway, the door opens before anybody reaches it and closes behind them, and nobody walks through anybody.

Verdict

Fixed in every room and on both devices: 0 of 1,920 walks touch anything, against 1,882 before. Checked on a Galaxy A15 in Kingsley's room.

Why it happened

Line numbers are in 5c6d204, the code before the fix.

  • There was no doorway. The back wall was one solid box (RoomDresser.cs:202) and the door a prefab slab in front of it (:234) that never moved. Nothing could walk through it.
  • The way out ended beside the door, against the wall. RoomLayout.ExitPoint (RoomLayout.cs:111) was at MinX + 1.8, 0.8 m right of the door's middle, and 0.1 m from the wall's centre. The last leg of every route ran along the wall at that depth, so a 0.34 m body was 0.39 m inside the wall and the door frame. M130 derived the door's place per room (:108), but the exit point was never moved onto it.
  • Out of a desk sideways. RoomLayout.Route (:338, :345) left and entered every seat along the row at the seat's own depth: through the bags beside the desks, and at shared tables (Eastgate, Brightpath, State University) through the neighbour sitting there.
  • The queue was walked through. The admission choreographer planned the walk to a desk and the walk out from the check-in spot as if nobody were standing behind it (AdmissionChoreographer.cs:333, :340). The queue stands exactly there.
  • The next person walked back to the door first. The person stepping up from the queue got the walk from the door to the desk (:317) while standing 1.15 m from the desk. They went back to the door's corner, through the desks, and came back: 3.3 to 7.4 s instead of half a second.
  • The queue was a straight line from the door. A queue place was a single point (:321), so people walked straight to it through chairs and desks.
  • The body walked 0.25 m ahead of every route. The standing and walking clips put the hips 0.25 m in front of the root (measured in the room: 0.249 m walking). Routes are planned for the root, so the body overshot every line by 0.25 m. At the door that is the whole wall's depth; standing up, it slid the body toward the desk.
  • An earlier fix never reached the rooms. The published version of this report described a door that opened. That work is on the branches removal-walkout and door-queue, made for the old single classroom before M130, and was never merged.

How they walk now

  • Lanes. Every walk follows the room's clear floor: the aisles, the gap behind each row (half way between the seated backs and the next row's desks), the cross-aisle at the back, and the door lane through the middle of the doorway. The shortest way along them is taken. Every room's lanes are worked out from its seats, so all eight rooms and both devices get theirs.
  • Out of a desk backwards. A student leaves their desk straight back into the gap behind their row, and comes into a desk the same way.
  • Past people standing. Somebody leaving the check-in spot does not take a lane past the people queuing if there is another: the next aisle, or, in rooms of long tables where the check-in spot is on a side aisle, a passing lane between the queue and the wall. The Eastgate coat rail moved to the right wall to make room for it.
  • Waiting and going round. A walker never steps into somebody walking or standing. Held up for half a second, it looks for another way round them. Of two people in each other's way, only the one who set off later turns. After 2.5 s it walks on, so the room can never stall.
  • The doorway, one way at a time. People going the same way follow each other through. Somebody coming in waits outside, out of sight, while somebody goes out, and the other way round.
  • The body over its root. The standing and walking poses now draw the body over the point the walk plans (0.001 m ahead on average, measured), blended in as the student stands up. Seated poses are unchanged.

The door

  • A real doorway. The back wall is built either side of a 0.92 m opening and above it. The door's slab became two jambs and a head, and the leaf and handle hang from a hinge on the left.
  • Open before anyone gets there. A walker asks for the door a few steps ahead: its pace times the 0.45 s swing, plus the leaf's reach. Nobody may be where the leaf swings, or in the doorway, unless it is wide open. If they arrive early they wait there. Checked every frame from every seat, at 60 and 10 frames a second.
  • Closed behind them. When nobody holds it, it swings shut after 0.3 s. Somebody leaving steps out and right along the corridor, beyond the leaf's reach, before they are switched off.
  • It swings out. The leaf opens into the corridor, so it never sweeps the room. Wide open it lies along the corridor's left side, with 8 cm to the walker's shoulder.
  • Reduce motion. The door is simply open or shut, with no swing. A removal still has no walk, as before.

Tests

  • Every walk clears everything (RoomWalkClearanceTests): the 1,920 walks above, against everything in the room. The same check of the old routes finds 1,882 touching.
  • The built room is the measured room (WalkDoorTests): every renderer the room builder makes at body height lies inside one of the footprints the clearance test uses, in every room on both devices.
  • The door before the body (WalkDoorTests): a removal from every seat of every room, at 60 and 10 frames a second. Wide open whenever the body is in its swing, through the middle of the doorway, shut behind them, nobody still holding it.
  • One way at a time, and nobody through anybody (WalkDoorTests): somebody coming in and somebody going out meet at the door. A walker waits for somebody standing in its way, and is never stopped for good.
  • In the real room (WalkPathSceneTests): a whole admission with a rejection, then a removal, in the Classroom scene. On every frame: no body in a wall, desk or the door, nobody in anybody, nobody in the doorway unless the door is wide open. The next person reaches the desk within the stamp's time plus 1.5 s; the walking body is over its root.
  • Everything else: the full EditMode suite (2,506 tests, 0 failed, 211 skipped) and the full PlayMode suite (110 tests, 74 passed, 0 failed, 36 skipped (the explicit capture tests)) pass. No fingerprint changed.

Not covered

  • Bathroom trips and the end of the exam have no walk. A student going to the bathroom switches off where they sit and comes back at their desk, and at the bell everybody stays seated. Nothing walks, so nothing collides. Giving them a walk would be a new feature.
  • One room on the phone. The phone check was in Kingsley's room only; every room was checked in the editor.
  • Head-on in an aisle. If two walkers meet head-on and neither can find another way round, they walk on after 2.5 s. No walk in the tested set does this.
  • Own chair. Standing up and sitting down still pass the student's own chair, as they always have.
