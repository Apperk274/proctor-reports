Proctor Base Comparison

Four ways to give the player's proctor a body whose face, hair, build and accessories can be customised from preset parts and colour swatches, set beside today's body. Students keep today's body, so every candidate is shown in the same flat-shaded classroom next to the current students. All images are renders from this spike: Blender 5.2, EEVEE, flat colours, a camera at the game's 58° pitch and 45° field of view.

Decision: B, the Quaternius kit

This spike recommended C (a new head on today's body). The owner chose B, the Quaternius modular kit, to get rid of the dummy look. The proctor built on it is in the Proctor Character report. The rest of this page is the record of the comparison as it was made.

Recommendation: C, upgrade in place

Keep the proctor's skeleton, clips and body, and replace only the head with a kit of rigid preset parts on the head bone: head shape, ears, nose, eyes, brows, mouth and facial hair, coloured by skin, hair and eye swatches. Add body builds as one shape key per body, made by a deformation field that is applied to the torso garments too.

Why C

  • Same rig and clips: Standing, Walking and PresentDocument play unchanged.
  • All 52 wardrobe meshes still fit: the new head keeps the old head's outer envelope, so hair, hats and glasses sit where they did. Polo, lanyard, blazer, cardigan, sweater and both glasses are shown on C below.
  • It stays the same art family as the students, at 2.5k–4k triangles.
  • The new parts are swapped the way hair and glasses already are in SupervisorFigure.

What C does not fix by itself

  • The body is still today's body. Hands are paddles and the limbs are simple prisms. A second pass can add hands and a softer torso on the same bones.
  • The parts in this spike are a scripted first draft. Noses and brows need an art pass, and ears are almost hidden under most hairstyles.
  • The proctor needs its own headless body export, because the students' body keeps its head.

When to pick something else

  • B if the proctor should look clearly more detailed than the students, and refitting the wardrobe to a new body is acceptable.
  • A only if near-realistic faces are the goal. Its proportions and faces read as a different game next to the students.

What the proctor animates with today

SupervisorFigure mixes three clips from candidate.glb on a manual PlayableGraph: Standing (idle), Walking (in place) and PresentDocument (the reach). They are Generic clips imported by glTFast on the 24-bone rig, not a Unity Humanoid avatar. Any new skeleton therefore means retargeting those three clips in Blender (the project already has a free retargeter, retarget_motion.py), or moving the proctor to FBX with a Humanoid avatar.

What "refit" means here

wardrobe.glb holds 52 meshes: 12 tops, 14 lanyards, 5 hats, 6 hair-under-hat cuts, 3 shoes, 2 glasses, a watch, a badge pin and 8 crests. All but the badge parts are skinned to the 24-bone rig and shaped to today's body. A new body means re-shaping and re-weighting each one. Under C, they all still fit.

Body builds

C makes a build (slim, average, broad, heavy) by moving vertices with one function of position. The body and every torso garment get the same shape key, so a sweater follows the body. The heavier man and woman below have their tops shaped this way. This is automatic for the existing tops and lanyards.

At the same scale

Men, then women: today, C, A, B. Today's body and C share their proportions. A is realistic, with a smaller head and longer limbs. B sits between them.

Baseline

Today: the shared student body

The proctor today is the students' body, with their hairstyles, tops and wardrobe. Variation is skin, hair and clothing colour. The face is two dots.

Candidate A

MPFB2 (MakeHuman for Blender), stylised

A MakeHuman human made in MPFB 2.0.18 with the low-poly body proxies (male1591, female1605), the CC0 system assets for hair, brows, eyes, suits and shoes, and the game-engine rig. To fit the game, the clothes and hair were decimated (suit 15k → 2.4k triangles, shoes 3.3k → 300, hair 3.3k → 900), everything is flat-shaded, and each garment's texture was reduced to three flat colours. Faces are presets of MakeHuman's face targets: head shape, nose, chin, cheeks and mouth.

How preset faces would work

Each face preset is a set of target weights. Bake each preset's targets into one shape key on the body proxy (and on every hair that touches the face), export, and pick the preset in Unity by setting that blendshape to 100. Hair, brows and eyes are separate skinned meshes swapped like today's hair.

Costs

Proportions are realistic (head about 1/7.5 of height against about 1/5.5 for the students). The system assets have no facial hair. MakeHuman hair is made for alpha cards, and it turns into solid shells once flat-shaded. Builds are free (MakeHuman macros), but every garment must follow them, which means MHCLO-fitting the whole wardrobe.

Candidate B

Quaternius Ultimate Modular Men and Women

A CC0 low-poly kit in which every character is four skinned parts on one shared rig: head, body, legs and feet. Any head fits any outfit of the same rig. Here the proctor wears the Suit outfit, with heads taken from other characters and the skin, hair and brow materials recoloured with the game's swatches.

How preset faces would work

A head is one skinned mesh, so swapping a head is the same operation as swapping today's hair. Face and hair are one mesh with separate materials. Splitting each head by material gives about 10 faces and about 10 hairstyles per gender that can be combined, though some hair cuts need a fit pass on other skulls. Colours are per-material tints, as today.

Costs

About 6.5k triangles, 2.5× today. The rig differs from ours (62 bones with fingers, men's and women's rigs not identical in the Humanoid FBX set), so every wardrobe item needs refitting and PresentDocument needs retargeting. Faces are angular and dark-browed, a little harsher than the students. Hats and helmets are baked into some heads.

Candidate C · recommended

Upgrade in place: a modular head on today's body

The proctor keeps today's body, rig and clips. The skinned head is cut off, and a scripted head kit is parented to the head bone: a head shape (oval, square, round or long) built inside the old head's envelope, ears, a nose (button, straight or long), two-tone eyes with a colour swatch, brows (straight, arched, thick or angled), a mouth, and optional facial hair (moustache or beard). Hair, hats and glasses are today's wardrobe meshes, unchanged.

How preset parts would work

Every part is a small mesh weighted fully to the head bone, exported into the proctor's parts library next to the hairstyles. Choosing a nose or brows is the same show/hide and bone re-pointing that hair and glasses already use. Skin, hair and eye colours are per-renderer tints by role. Head shapes all share the same cranium, so every hairstyle and hat fits every shape.

Costs

Art: an art pass on the scripted parts, more of each kind, and hands. Code: new appearance fields (head shape, nose, brows, facial hair, eye colour, build) in the proctor's appearance and save data, a headless proctor body, build shape keys on torso garments in the export, and the picker UI. No retargeting, no wardrobe refit.

Not rendered in this spike

D · Quaternius Universal Base Characters

Likely the best third-party option on paper: a CC0 base body in regular and teen proportions with 20 separate hairstyles, a Humanoid rig built for retargeting, Unity URP support and compatibility with Quaternius's Universal Animation Library. It averages about 13k triangles. The free Standard download (122 MB) is only offered through itch.io's download page, which this spike could not script. If B's direction appeals, download it by hand and compare it next.

E · Meshy-generated base

Prepared, not sent. The request uses Meshy's smart-topology model meshy-t2.5 with a 4,000-polygon target and A-pose, which avoids the earlier failure where pose and polycount were never sent. It costs 5 credits per preview. No call was made, so nothing was spent. A generated whole body would still need rigging, retargeting and a wardrobe refit, as A and B do.

How these were made

  • All candidates were built by scripts in the game repo and rendered in Blender 5.2 with EEVEE: one sun, a soft fill, a neutral ambient and a dark blue backdrop like the Wardrobe preview.
  • Room view: the game's fixed camera, with 58° pitch and 45° vertical field of view, 9.5 m from the room centre, so that two desk columns fill a portrait phone frame as they do in the game's screenshots. The "closer" crop uses the same camera position with a 3× narrower field of view. The students are today's body playing the Writing clip.
  • Close-ups use 512 × 640 frames, the Wardrobe preview's size.
  • This is look development only. Nothing here is in the game.
