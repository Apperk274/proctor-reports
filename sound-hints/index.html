Sound Hints and Phone Detector

A heard noise now names its rows ("A phone buzzing - Row 2, left side") instead of a rough area ("front middle"), and the Signal Detector became the Phone Detector, which marks students. This report checks whether that made catching cheaters too easy. In short: the row hints are not easier, and they are fairer. The Detector does find the phone after a buzz, but in bot careers it does not raise catches.

How many students a hint points at

The old scheme (before 85db134) divided the room's full desk area into up to 3×3 parts, sized at one part per two students in the class. It did not account for which desks were filled. The new scheme (SoundSystem.RegionOf) names rows, counted from the front. A row with one person in it merges with the row next to it. A side is named only when every side holds two people. Both schemes were measured on the same seats.

The new rows are narrower than the old areas in big rooms, which used to give 4 to 7 students. They are wider where the old areas had one student, because front-first seating left one person in an area. A side is named on 31% of seatings, but on only 7% of hints actually heard. When only one student is in the room, the whole room is the region (0.9% of hints). That is the one documented exception.

Search effort

The bots do use hints. CareerRunner walks the bot to the middle of the region it heard, and then ordinary perception takes over. They never use the Detector, so for this report a variant bot was added in the harness only. It fires the Detector on every heard buzz while it has uses left, then walks to the first student named. For a player, a simple scan model works: walk to the region's middle at 2.1 m/s, then check students in a random order at about 2 s each.

Effect on catches

Every difference is within about 1.5%, and it changes sign between the two seed blocks. For example, average coins were old 1,207 / new 1,203 in one block and 1,246 / 1,208 in the other. A bot cannot exploit a hint because seeing is what counts. Walking 0.4 m closer, or straight to the right student, rarely changes whether the phone is out when you get there. The M130 coin drop is untouched.

Fairness: does a hint ever single one student out?

  • At the moment of the sound: no. Every one of the 16 rooms, every class size and every seating had a minimum of 2, and so did every hint heard in careers. The one exception is a room with one person present, where the whole room is the region.
  • Afterwards: yes, sometimes. The hint stays on screen for 25 s. In 8.2% of hints (old: 12.3%), its region later holds only one person present, for about 15 s, because someone was removed or left for the bathroom. For buzzes it is 13%, and in most of those cases the person left behind is not the one whose phone buzzed.
  • That was a real bug in the heard-buzz grounds, now fixed. HeardBuzzPointsAt counted only the people still in the region. Since a region always holds two at the sound, it fired only after someone left, and the person who left had often been removed for that very phone. Over 9,000 exams per bot, these grounds pointed at a student who had not made the sound 87% (careful) and 64% (average) of the time, against 45% and 43% under the old areas. Now HeardSound.LoneOccupantId records who was alone in the region at the moment of the sound, and only that counts. Afterwards: 0 grounds against a bystander. Bot careers are byte-identical, because the bots never warn on these grounds. Tests: B_AMateWhoLeavesAfterTheBuzzDoesNotLeaveTheOtherImplicated, AHeardSoundNamesALoneOccupantOnlyWhenTheRoomHeldOnePerson.
  • The Detector singles students out by design. After a buzz it names exactly one student 99% of the time. This is fair only because innocent phones give the same signal (80% of hits). A hit is Ambiguous grounds for a warning for 6 s, never evidence, and the phone must still be seen before it can be taken (§4, §8; M120 "no credit for a guess").

Recommendation

Keep the row hints. They are not easier than the old areas, and they removed a real fairness hole. The Detector was the lever for making things scarcer in the spirit of M130. It keeps its 2 uses, and it now costs 400¢ in the Store, so owning it is the decision. The hint options below are unchanged.

Phone Detector wording

A store audit found direction wording left over from the Signal Detector. The compass result text was already gone, and no player-facing string says "bearing". The Store tile still said "shows which nearby student", which suggests one student and does not describe the mark.

Before: "Shows which nearby student has an active phone. A lead, not proof."
Now: "Marks each nearby student with an active phone. A lead, not proof."

It fits the 4 lines the tile reserves at the largest text size (EveryDescriptionFitsItsBlockAtTheLargestTextSize). A new test pins the text and rules out direction wording. "2 uses per exam" is already on the tile. The result line ("Phone signal: Emre - Row 2, left side. A lead, not proof.") was already accurate.

Method

An out-of-Unity harness compiled the game's Core and Balance code at 450d7e4, with Unity's Roslyn and .NET 6. The harness-only patches were the old sector scheme (from 85db134^) and a Detector-using bot variant. Careers ran one at a time, 4 configurations × 2 bots × 2 seed blocks × 150 careers × 60 exams, starting at Maple on a phone. The static sweep used RoomCatalog, RoomLayout.Build and CandidateFactory.SpreadSeats, the same calls the room invariant test uses.
