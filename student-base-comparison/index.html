Student Base Comparison

Which base the exam students should be rebuilt on, so that every school's candidates read as their age (child, young teen, older teen, adult) and sit in the same style as the new proctor. Five candidates are rendered beside the proctor: standing at the same scale, seated at the game's one desk and chair, and in the door's ID-photo framing. All images are renders from this spike in Blender 5.2 (EEVEE), with the proctor renders' sun, fill and backdrop. Nothing here is in the game.

Recommendation (the owner decides)

Build every student on A, the proctor's Ultimate Modular kit, with age shaping. That keeps one rig contract and the proctor's exact look. For the child band, add a dedicated child head set on the kit's head bone, because a shrunken adult face does not read as 7 to 10. C (MPFB) is the reference for that head set, and the fallback for children only. Details are under Recommendation.

Why A

  • It is the proctor's own kit, so students and proctor are the same style by construction.
  • The proctor's pipeline already handles it: role colours, wardrobe refit, sealing, build shapes and badges.
  • Age shaping is a bake. The rig is posed with scaled bones and the pose becomes the new rest pose, so every band keeps the same bone names and hierarchy, and one retarget map serves all of them.
  • The same student clip retarget works on all four bands with no per-band tuning (see Retarget).

Main risks

  • Budget. A dressed student is 5.9k–7.5k triangles in 8–10 meshes with 11–14 role materials. Reaching 2.5k–3k in 1–2 skinned renderers needs authored low parts: mitten hands, low hair and merged parts. Naive decimation tears the neck and face (Budget).
  • Child read. This depends on art that does not exist yet: the child head set.
  • Desk contact. Shorter arms miss the desk: a child's wrists end 3 cm inside the desk edge. This needs a contact pass per band, baked or runtime IK.
  • Material count. The kit has one material per role. Students need one material: a palette atlas, or the proctor's per-submesh MaterialPropertyBlock approach, if the A15's GPU budget allows it.

When to pick something else

  • C (MPFB) for every band if a convincing age read matters more than matching the proctor. It has the best age read and one rig across all ages, but realistic proportions and textured clothes read as a different game next to the proctor, and all wardrobe would be new work.
  • Not B. The free Universal Base Characters are two adult superhero bodies with no clothes and about 16k–18k triangles. The teen bodies are only in the paid Source edition.
  • Not D. The free child bases found are either chibi (Gobkit) or crude and uncoloured (OGA Lowpoly Kid).

What 2.5k triangles costs A

The same A students with every part collapsed to a per-part budget (body 520, face 360, hair 380, top 640, legs 360, shoes 170, brows 60): 2,342 to 2,558 triangles. This is an automatic decimation, not the proposed art.

Retargeting the student clips

Each figure takes the student clips from candidate.glb (the 24-bone rig). The spike retargets 20 bones (hips, three spine bones, neck, head, both arms and both legs) with one script for all three rigs: the kit (62 bones), MPFB (53) and UBC (65). Each bone copies the clip bone's world rotation relative to rest. The rest frames are aligned on the bone's direction and the character's forward axis, so twist is fixed. The hips come from the clip, then the body is dropped until it rests on the 0.475 m seat.

What works

  • Writing, HandRaised and Standing hold on every band and rig with no per-band tuning: spine lean, shoulders, elbows and the raised arm all read.
  • Small bodies sit on the same chair with their feet hanging (child 14 cm off the floor). Bodies fit the desk; the desk stays the same.
  • The proctor's existing bone map (proctor_parts.RETARGET) extends to the legs with four more pairs per side.

What breaks

  • Contact. Copying rotations keeps the adult's hand placement only for adult-length arms. A child's wrists end 3 cm in the desk and 6 cm short of its edge; an older teen's hover 5 cm above it. The tallest adults put their toes up to 7 cm through the floor.
  • The kit's feet hang from Root, not the shins. They must be placed at the shin's end every frame, or the ankle stretches.
  • Twist. Aligning on bone direction alone tore the MPFB faces; aligning whole frames fixed it.
  • MPFB, headless. Without MakeHuman's joint helper groups, every age gets the adult skeleton. With them, the rig fits each age.
  • Fingers are not in the source. The kit takes them from its idle; MPFB and UBC hands stay flat.

Effort for the 18 motions

  • Batch retarget of all clips and mirrors onto the chosen rig: about 1 day, from this spike's script.
  • Contact per band for the clips that touch the desk, lap or bag (about 10 of 18): either bake 4 bands × 18 clips (plus mirrors) through an extended correct_clip.py, about 1.5–2 weeks with review, or ship one clip set plus runtime two-bone IK on the hands against a per-band desk plane and foot plant, about 1 week of code and tests.
  • Review renders per band with pose_check.py: 2–3 days.

How these were made

  • Headless Blender 5.2, EEVEE, with the proctor render helpers: one sun, a soft fill, neutral ambient and the dark blue backdrop. Flat shading throughout, except B, which keeps its own textures.
  • A's age shaping: the proctor's parts are posed with scaled bones (Root to band height; head grown to 0.95, 0.965 or 0.985 of the adult head's size; neck shortened; legs and hands slightly smaller) and baked into a new rest pose, height-fitted in three passes. The face gets a position warp: shorter lower face, nose pulled back, jaw narrowed, cheeks fuller and eyes enlarged, strongest for the child. Builds use the kit's own slim and heavy shapes.
  • C: MPFB's age, gender and weight macros; the 1591- and 1605-vertex body proxies; hair, clothes and shoes decimated to 900, 1,400 and 300 triangles; clothes recoloured to three flat colours sampled from their textures.
  • Door close-ups frame on the eyes at three times the eye-to-crown height, 512 × 640, as in the Wardrobe preview. Room views use the game camera.
  • This is a decision spike. Nothing in the game repo was changed.
