Summary

The ten problems that matter most

The game is consistent and themed, and its colours and buttons already follow one system. What it lacks is hierarchy and payoff. Most screens are long scrolls of equally weighted cards, events are told in paragraphs, and the best reward moment in the game happens on a different screen from the one the player is looking at.

The salary is a line of text broken feel

The result card shows "Salary +50 ¢", which counts up. The coins only fly later, on the hub, from mid-screen. When expenses are due (every 4th exam) no coins fly at all. Diagnosis.

No headline on the result

Good, Mixed or Poor is computed but never shown. The card opens on "Exam complete", which tells the player nothing about how the exam went.

The Store is one 5-screen scroll

Tools, medicine and the wardrobe share one page: 172 labels, 406 words and 80 buttons, with two nested rows of segmented controls inside the wardrobe.

Events are told in paragraphs

Event cards run 40 to 58 words, with a 30 to 40 word body. The price and the stakes sit in the middle of prose ("…something for 120 coins - otherwise…").

The hub repeats itself and then goes empty

The rank and posting appear in the top bar and again in the hero. Below the next-exam card there is a large empty area, then one big button. Nothing on the hub is tappable apart from that button.

The Manual is a tab

868 words of prose get a permanent quarter of the tab bar, and the Manual is also linked from Settings. Rules the player needs at a given moment belong on that moment's screen.

Tab names do not say what is behind them

"Exam" opens the home hub, and "Career" opens a ledger that repeats the hub. The gear opens Settings, or the pause screen during an exam, and while Settings is open no tab is lit.

Onboarding is three text walls

Three cards of 29 to 43 words each, with no picture, before the first tap that matters. The ideas in them are good, but they arrive as reading.

The admission desk is a data table

75 words: ten rows comparing document against exam in small type, plus the rules in prose. It is the game's core puzzle, presented as a form.

Small visual bugs polish

The rank line in the top bar is clipped at the top on every screen. A notice from the exam can sit over the result card. On the first frame the hub opens, its text overlaps.

Diagnosis

Why the salary reads as "+120" and not as coins

The coin flight works as written: in a real flow, 4 coins flew and the counter went 0 → 50 ¢. It is designed to happen somewhere the player is not looking, and it is skipped on the exams with the biggest money swing. Reduce motion is off on this Mac's save, and nothing on the player's side turns it off.

  1. The coin counter is hidden on the result card, so nothing can fly there. HudRouter.cs:323-325 hides the chrome whenever a boundary card, an event or the door is up (bool modal = _ui.Boundary.Visible || …). The design guide says so too (UI-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md:220: "the salary is paid in when the player returns to the hub").
  2. On the result card, the salary is a text row. HudRouter.AddPaymentRows (:1579) makes the row "Salary" with trailing text "+{0} ¢". BoundaryView.CountIn (:285-299) counts it up from 0 and pops the row icon once. No coin is drawn and no stack forms. The "¢" glyph stands in for money.
  3. The flight plays on the hub, from the middle of the screen. ChromeView.AnimateCoins (:309-347) uses Vector3 source = _root.TransformPoint(Vector3.zero) (:326), the screen centre, because the payment row it came from no longer exists. The hub's entrance plays in the same 0.26 s, and one or more event cards can sit between the result and the hub. By then, cause and effect are screens apart.
  4. It flies the net change, and no coins fly when that is negative. The chrome compares its last balance with the new one (:317: if (delta <= 0 …) roll down). Every 4th exam (CareerService.cs:511, ExpenseCadenceExams = 4) monthly expenses of 220 ± 60 per tier outweigh a salary of 50–190. On those exams the player never sees the salary arrive: the balance simply rolls down. Store spending since the last hub visit is netted in the same way. Recorded: 1150 → 1040, with no flight.
  5. A small salary looks like decoration. The flight sends one coin per 15 earned, clamped to 3–8 (:325). The Maple salary of 50 sends 4 glyphs, in the Money green, with no gold and no stack.

Fix (Phase 1). Pay out on the result card itself:

  • Show a read-only coin pill on the result card.
  • Fly gold coins from each income row into a visible stack, then into the pill.
  • Play each payment as its own beat: income in, then costs out. A salary is always seen, even when the month's bills take more.
  • The hub then has nothing left to animate.

What the numbers say

The result card is not the text problem. It has 17 words; its problem is that it has no payoff. The words are in the Manual (868), the Store (406, with 80 buttons), the Career tab (160), and the event cards and intro cards, which carry 30 to 40 word bodies.

The best screens are already the shortest: the exam HUD (6 words), the two-school offer (numbers in columns) and the parking fine (one line plus the price).

Worst offenders

1. Manual: 868 words, one 54-word paragraph.
2. Store: 406 words, 80 buttons, 5 screens of scroll.
3. Career tab: 160 words, repeating hub and top-bar facts.
4. Reassignment letter: 40-word body.
5. Manager meeting: 38-word body.
6. Intro card 2: 36-word body.
7. Admission desk: 75 words in a 10-row table.

Navigation map

How the screens connect

There are two kinds of screen. Tabs can be browsed freely. Modal cards (result, events, brief, door) have to be answered, and the chrome hides while they are up. The loop between two exams passes through up to seven modal cards and the hub.

tab (chrome visible)modal card (chrome hidden, must answer)the roomend of career
1tap from the hub into the next exam, plus 1 per pre-exam event and 1 per candidate at the door (typically 4–9)
2–5taps from the bell to the hub: Continue, then one per post-exam event (observed: Continue, OK, OK, OK)
2 + 3 swipesto buy clothing: the Store tab, scroll past the tools and medicine, filter, Buy
1to check the career, but the school ladder is the 3rd card down
5taps from New career to the first room: 3 intro cards and the hub button

Dead ends and unclear paths

• "Exam" tab = home. The tab is named after a place the player is not yet in; the hub's button says "Go to next exam".
• Settings lights no tab and has no Back. The way out is to pick a tab, or Android back.
• The Manual has two doors (the tab and a Settings row) and is the only tab that never changes.
• The gear means two things: Settings on the hub, Pause in the room.
• Dismissed is a true dead end by design. That is correct, but it is the only screen with no recap of the career.

What works

• The persistent top bar and tab bar replaced a menu tree; Back is gone where the bar is visible.
• Answer-before-browse on events is a fairness rule and stays.
• Android back is handled by one policy (BackNavigation).
• The modal sheet layout (picture, kicker, title, body, buttons) is consistent everywhere.

References

What to borrow, and what to do better

These draw on New Star Soccer (mobile), which the player named, and on current mobile game UI. Web sources say little about NSS's hub layout. Its 2016 v2 update gave the menus "a complete graphical facelift" that "streamlined navigation". The notes below are about patterns. No assets are copied.

New Star Soccer: borrow

  • One obvious next action. A big "next match" button anchors the career screen. Ours is "Go to next exam", but it floats under an empty area.
  • Relationships as bars, not sentences. NSS shows each relationship as a short labelled bar. We have reputation as "42 / 100" and probation as prose.
  • Money as a number you watch grow. Wages and bonuses are counted in and spent in a lifestyle shop.
  • Life events as short messages with two answers. Our event cards have the same shape; ours just say too much.

New Star Soccer: do better

  • Its menus are dated, by its own players' and reviewers' account: lists, small type and dense stat pages. Our base is already more polished.
  • No illustration. We have baked school and event art, so lead with it.
  • Feedback is mostly numbers changing. We can make money and standing physical: coins, stars and a stack you can see.

2026 mobile patterns worth using

  • A bottom tab bar as primary navigation, per Apple's HIG and Material 3, with 3–5 nouns and the active tab filled.
  • Hub of big tiles. Each tile has one icon, one word and one number or badge. Badges carry "new" or "affordable".
  • Bold numbers. A currency or score is the biggest type on its card, with tabular numerals and icons beside them.
  • Reward choreography: a burst, then a flight to the counter, which ticks per coin and pops once at the end. The source and the counter are on screen together.
  • Card stacks for decisions: one card at a time, illustration first, two big answers.
  • Icon chips instead of rule sentences, as in Clash Royale and Brawl Stars, with "ⓘ" for detail on demand.

Sources: TouchArcade on NSS 2.0 · Pocket Gamer: 5 ways NSS 2.0 feels new · Macworld review · Pixune: best mobile game UI (2026) · Elinext: 2026 mobile UI trends. NSS screen specifics (bars, button, shop) are from the game as commonly played, not from these articles.

Proposal

Simple to read, warm to look at, and every reward on screen

These directions stay inside the system: every value below becomes a GameTheme token, and every block is a Ui component that screens ask for by role. The rules that stay are no pulsing, Reduce motion landing everything at once, the 3D room never hinting at guilt, and a choice card never predicting a hidden outcome. A choice may show only a cost the player is already told, such as "120".

Text rules

≤ 4words in a title or a button. A button is a verb, and the player's cost goes in a chip.
≤ 14words in a card body; one sentence. The rest goes behind ⓘ, or into the Manual.
1number per fact. A balance appears once on a screen, in the top bar.
Icon + numberbefore icon + sentence: coins, stars, candidates, rules, uses.
0"¢" glyphs. The coin is an icon, gold, and the same icon everywhere.
1primary action per screen, and it is the biggest thing below the fold.

Store, exam HUD and event card

Reward and feedback motion set

Keep CountUp, Punch, FlightLayer, MeterFill, Shimmer, Reveal and the toasts. Add, with new tokens on the existing timings:

• Payout: income row bursts gold coins, they arc to a pill on the same screen and tick it per coin, then one pop. FlightLayer.Fly(fromRow) as planned in M129.
• Stack: coins drop onto a pile (OutBack, 0.32 s, 80 ms stagger, max 8) before the pour. New CoinStack component.
• Spend: one coin flies out to the bought tile. No celebration.
• Outcome chip: scale 0.9 → 1 with alpha, once.
• Promotion: keep the sweep and add the salary "50 → 60" count.
• Warn flinch (3D): a 0.3 s look-up for everyone warned, guilty or not.
• Reduce motion: every one lands in its first frame. Nothing pulses or loops; there is no shake and no confetti.

Phased plan

Payday first, then the hub, then everything else

Each phase ships on its own, with captures and a device check on the A15. The estimates are agent-days of implementation and verification, assuming hub option B. Options A and C add about 2 days to Phase 2.

How this was made. Assets/Tests/PlayMode/UiOverhaulCaptures.cs (PlayMode, Explicit) drives a new career in batchmode. It moves the overlay canvas onto the main camera and renders 1080 × 2340, 720 × 1560 and a 540 × 1170 frame recorder. On every shot it writes a text audit of the live UI tree. Some screens are staged (title, rare events, offers, dismissal); everything else is the router's own flow. The editor's save files were backed up and verified unchanged afterwards. The motion log, audit JSON and frames are kept locally under DiagnosticOutput/UiOverhaul/ on the development machine (not committed).
