Summary

The ten problems that matter most

The game is consistent and themed, and its colours and buttons already follow one system. What it lacks is hierarchy and payoff. Most screens are long scrolls of equally weighted cards, events are told in paragraphs, and the best reward moment in the game happens on a different screen from the one the player is looking at.

The salary was a line of text fixed in Phase 1

Gold coins now drop onto a pile on the result card and pour into the card's own balance. Bills leave after the pour. Phase 1: Payday.

No headline on the result fixed in Phase 1

A Good, Mixed or Poor chip now sits under "Exam complete".

The Store was one 5-screen scroll fixed in Phase 3

The Shop and the Wardrobe are two screens, each with one row of segments, tiles with a price chip and a pick-then-Buy sheet. 12 to 40 words a segment. Phase 3: Shop.

Events were told in paragraphs fixed in Phase 4

A card is now one sentence of 14 words at most, with its facts as chips and a known cost on its answer ("Fix it (coin) 80"). Cards average 20 words, down from 33. Phase 4: Words.

The hub repeated itself and then went empty fixed in Phase 2

The rank is on the status bar only and the picture names the place. Start sits under the picture, and four tiles fill the rest. Phase 2: Home.

The Manual was a tab fixed in Phase 2

It is now the Rules screen behind the Rules tile. It opens with the next exam's own rules. The Settings row opens the same screen. Since Phase 4 it is short rows that open, 251 words with every row closed.

Tab names did not say what was behind them fixed in Phase 2

No tab bar any more. Each tile says where it goes and one live fact about it. The gear means Settings only; in the room the control is a pause button.

Onboarding was three text walls words fixed in Phase 4

The three cards are one sentence each now, 16 to 20 words a card (were 29 to 43). They still have no picture of their own.

The admission desk was a data table fixed in Phase 5

The person and their ID are two cards side by side, each field a glyph and a value, the rules are chips, and a decision is stamped onto the cards. 51 words, down from 72. Phase 5: Room.

Small visual bugs 2 of 3 fixed

The notice that sat over the result card and the hub's first-frame overlap are fixed. The clipped rank line could not be reproduced. Details.

Phase 1 · Payday · shipped

The salary is paid in on the result card, in gold

The result card is now option B, the payday receipt. It has its own balance pill and a short receipt of what came in and what went out. The salary drops onto a small pile of gold coins, the pile pours into the balance, and then any bill leaves it calmly. The status bar on the hub already shows the same number, so nothing is paid twice.

How it plays

  1. The outcome chip arrives. Good, Mixed or Poor, from the same classification the career log already uses ("Exam 3 finished – mixed"). Good is green, Mixed is neutral, and Poor uses the warning colour. It scales from 0.9 and fades in, once.
  2. Coins drop onto a pile beside the income line. There is one coin per 15 earned, with at least 3 and at most 10, so a 50 salary is a short stack and a 190 salary is a heap. The centre column leads, so even 3 coins read as a stack. While they fall, the line's own number counts up to +50.
  3. The pile pours into the balance pill. The top coin leaves first, along an arc. The pill counts up as each coin lands, with a soft tap on every other coin, and pops once on the last.
  4. Costs leave after the pour. One calm roll down, with no pop, no flight, no shake and no flash. A salary is never netted away.
  • A tap anywhere lands everything at once: the pile is poured and the balance shows its true number. The tap still reaches what is under it, so Continue works at any moment.
  • Reduce motion shows the end state in the first frame.
  • The payday plays once per exam. A redraw of the open card, or a theme switch that rebuilds it, shows the end state.
  • The card ends on the balance after the bill. The monthly bill settles without a screen once the result is read, so the card already lists it. That is the number the status bar takes when the hub returns (ChromeView.Settle).
  • The status bar does not pay it again. In the clip below, the bar reads 50 as soon as the hub appears, and no coins fly.

The gold coin

Coins are now gold everywhere they appear as an object: the balance pill in the status bar and on the card, every receipt line, every coin in flight, and the pile. The coin is drawn in code in layers that the theme colours: a lit face, a darker rim with one inner ring, and a highlight. The pile uses a side view of the same coin. There is no "¢" next to a balance, because the coin is the unit. A gain is still written in green (+50) and a cost in the danger colour (-160). In the light theme, the balance number uses a dark gold that clears 4.5:1. Phase 2 took "¢" off the Career screen, Phase 3 off every price in the Shop and the Wardrobe, and Phase 4 off everything left; a test now fails on one.

The three visual bugs from Phase 0

  • The exam notice over the result card: fixed. A notice raised in the room now ends when the exam does. A notice raised anywhere else, such as the save-recovery line at launch, still gets its full 8 seconds.
  • The hub's first-frame overlap: fixed. The overlapping text was the previous hub's cards. Destroy is deferred to the end of the frame, so the old cards stayed in the layout while the new ones were laid out, and both were drawn. A screen now deactivates what it discards (Ui.Discard) before destroying it. This applies to the hub, the boundary cards and the panel screens.
  • The clipped rank line: not reproduced. The Phase 0 captures, the new captures and the phone at the largest text size all show the top bar's rank and posting fully inside the bar: about 30 px clear above and 26 px below at 1080 wide. Nothing in the code clips it. If it shows up again, it needs a screenshot from the device where it happened.

Checked

New tests check that the balance ends on the economy's own number on a normal exam, on a bill exam that leaves the player in debt, and on an exam that paid nothing. They also check that the salary is visibly counted in before the bill leaves, that a tap mid-pour lands everything, that Reduce motion shows the end in the first frame, and that the hub does not pay the salary again. Two guards check that a payday moves nothing the layout owns and that no payday timing is written as a number.
  • On the phone (Galaxy A15). The dev build was installed with adb install -r and played on a real bill exam. A car repair (−80) was paid that morning, then Salary +140 and the monthly bill −280. The balance shows 812, a heap of 10 gold coins pours in to 952, then rolls down to 592, which matches the economy. The coins read as gold coins at phone size. During the coin animation the screen recording's longest gap between frames was 34 ms. The only long gaps, 168 ms and 110 ms, fall on the frame the exam ends and the card is built. After the exam before it (Salary +140 to 812), the hub's bar read 812 straight away and nothing flew. The phone's save files were restored afterwards and their hashes checked.
What was wrong before (Phase 0 diagnosis, superseded)
  • The coin counter was hidden on the result card, so the salary was paid in later, on the hub, from the middle of the screen. Several event cards could sit in between.
  • On the card, the salary was a line of text: Salary +50 ¢.
  • Only the net change flew. Every 4th exam the bills outweighed the salary, and the balance simply rolled down, so the salary was never seen.
  • The flying coins were thin green outline glyphs, and a 50 salary sent four of them.
Phase 2 · Home · shipped

Home is one big button and four tiles

The hub is now option A. At the top is the status bar: the rank as a brass chip, reputation, the gold coin balance and the gear. Below it is the picture of where you work, with its name and stars. Then comes Start exam N, with one line saying the kind of exam and how many candidates. Four tiles fill the rest of the screen: Career, Shop, Wardrobe and Rules. Each tile has an icon, a short name and one live fact. Each opens a full screen with a × that leads back. The tab bar is gone. The hub reads in 34 words, down from 47, and carries no rule sentences.

What each tile says

  • Career: the way to the next school, "5 to Riverside" (reputation points to go). On probation it reads "Probation: 2 left", and under a final warning "Final warning", both in the warning colour. At the top posting it reads "Top posting". The Career screen's next-posting bar reads the same method (CareerPresentation.NextPosting), so the tile and the bar can never disagree. Since Phase 3 it also knows M134's minimum stay: "4 more exams here" until the stay is served.
  • Shop: tools and medicine you could buy now, "2 you can afford", using the same rules as the Shop's buttons. Otherwise how far off the cheapest one is ("40 coins short"), or "All owned".
  • Wardrobe: clothes you could buy now, otherwise how many you own ("12 of 31 owned"), or "All owned". (The mockup said "2 new". The game does not track what you have seen, so the fact counts what you can buy.)
  • Rules: what the next exam allows at the desk, as a count ("1 item allowed", "Nothing allowed"). The sentence moved to the Rules screen, where it comes first.

Every screen a tile opens

The promotion says what it pays

It plays once, on the first hub that shows the new school. Any redraw after that, such as a purchase, no longer carries it. With Reduce motion on, the new figure is simply there.

How it moves

  • Screen changes are a crossfade, alpha only. A screen fades in over the hub in 0.16 s (PanelFadeSeconds) and fades out on close. It stops taking taps at once. Moving from one full screen to another (Career, then the gear) does not fade; the cards' entrance does that.
  • The hub stays standing under a screen it opened. Closing a screen reveals the hub that was already there. The 3D room stays undrawn behind it. The hub is not repainted unless something it shows changed.
  • The tiles arrive one after another the first time the hub opens, never on a redraw. They scale and fade, and nothing moves position; a test watches the tiles' positions through the entrance.
  • Tiles press like buttons, using the same press feedback as every other control. Nothing loops or pulses.
  • Reduce motion now also holds the entrance and the fade: a screen is simply there.
  • The picture and the tiles share the screen. The picture grows to its natural height first, then the tiles up to a ceiling (TileMaxHeight), then the picture again. A tall phone shows more of the place rather than emptier tiles, and the hub never ends in an empty band.

Where the brief left room

  • × in the top-left of the header, beside the title, where a full-screen dialog keeps it. The status bar's gear stays top-right, so the two never sit on top of each other.
  • The status bar stays on every tile screen, because the Shop and the Wardrobe need the balance. The gear hides while Settings is open. The bar hides while an exam is under way: in the room, on the pause screen and on the resume hub.
  • Shop and Wardrobe are two screens, not one page scrolled to a section. The split was cheap: the Store builds one part or the other. Phase 3 redesigned both.
  • The Settings row to the Rules stays, because it is the only way to the Rules from the title screen. Both doors open the same screen with the same name, and its × goes back to whichever door you used.
  • In the room the gear became a pause glyph, because it opens "Exam paused". The gear now means Settings and nothing else.
  • The onboarding tip stays on the hub for the first few exams as one slim card under the tiles. It is not counted in the 34 words.
  • Probation moved into the Career tile's fact, in the warning colour, and into a bar on the Career screen. The probation card on the hub is gone.
  • The Career screen is now three bars (reputation, next posting, and probation when it applies), the ladder with each school's pay as a gold coin, and the last three things that happened. The overview card and the coin-by-coin ledger, which repeated the status bar, are gone.

Phase 3 · Shop · shipped

Pick a tile, then Buy

The Shop and the Wardrobe are no longer one long scroll. Each has one row of segments and a grid of tiles. A tile is the item, its name and one chip: the price as a gold coin and a number, what is still missing ("210 short"), what it is ("Owned", "Carried", "Worn") or what it waits for (a lock and "4 / 6"). Tapping a tile picks it, and a sheet comes up at the foot with one line and the one thing to do: Buy with its price, Carry, Wear or Take off. Buying is always two taps, in both screens. The balance is in the status bar only. When you buy, one gold coin flies from that balance to the tile, the balance rolls down, and the tile pops once as its new chip fades in. There is no "¢" anywhere in either screen.

The Wardrobe

Crests are earned, not bought

  • No crest is sold. Each one is earned by exams at its school: 6 at Maple, Riverside and Eastgate, 10 everywhere else. The numbers come from the career (CareerService.ExamsAt, CrestExamsRequiredAt); the screen never writes a 6 or a 10 itself.
  • The lines are honest. A crest you are working towards reads "4 / 6 exams at Riverside". A school you have never worked at reads "Work 10 exams at Brightpath". Careful careers never reach Brightpath or State University, so the text never suggests that you will.
  • The award is an ordinary reward card, "Riverside Crest earned", with the crest's own picture and one six-word line. It never opens the "watch an ad for a bonus" offer; every other reward still does.
  • A crest you already bought stays yours, worn like any pin. Nothing is refunded.
  • Old saves (v6 → v7). Crests are earned by exams at a school, and M134 only started counting when a save loaded. So a long career would have found the crests of every school it had left out of reach. The migration counts the exams the save records. It uses each exam's room where there is one (since M130). Otherwise it uses the career's own moves: each accepted offer or reassignment, at the exam it was answered. An offer from before M129 names no school left, and every career then started at City High. An exam that cannot be placed is not counted, so nothing is invented. A crest already earned arrives as its award card, once. The long career on the phone (Kingsley, exam 11, saved by a build from before v3) loads with nothing lost: City High 9 exams, Kingsley 2, and no card, because 9 is short of 10.

Phase 4 · Words · shipped

One sentence, and the numbers as chips

The goal was: "We should not choke the user with many texts; each text should be concise and understandable easily." Every event card is now a picture, a kicker of three words at most, a title of one or two words, one sentence of at most fourteen words, its facts as chips, and answers that are verbs. A cost the card has told you sits on the answer as a gold coin and a number. A card whose consequences are hidden has no chip on any answer. The pre-exam brief shows the exam's rules as chips. The Rules screen is a list of short rows that open. These limits are enforced: a test reads every player-facing string, and the card template checks its own slots. No "¢" is left anywhere.

The event card

  • The template. Picture · KICKER (≤ 3 words) · Title (≤ 4, usually one or two, on one line) · the speaker, when the game knows one · "Earlier: you removed …" (≤ 10) · one sentence (≤ 14 words, no number in it) · the facts as chips · the answers (≤ 4 words, a verb). EventSceneView checks each slot and reports any that are over in the editor and in development builds. A test checks every wording of every card kind.
  • Known costs on the answer. These are the repair, the medicine, the fine and the bills on Pay, the pay for covering a room (+) on Accept, and a coin reward (+) on Claim. Only amounts the card has already told you get a chip.
  • No chips when the outcome is hidden. This covers the bribe, the refund demand, blackmail, the manager meeting, the coworker's favour, the door, the national exam and a witness interview. A priced answer beside an unpriced one would hint which way the hidden part goes. Fairness comes first: when such a card names a sum ("150 offered", "120 demanded"), the sum is still on the card as a fact chip.
  • The red "Cost" box is now a chip. What a finding takes from your standing is a star and "-4". The national exam's stake is two chips, "+200 bonus" and "Closely watched", under one sentence that says the bonus is paid if it goes well.
  • The card arrives by fading in (it used to rise 70 px). The fade and the hold after an answer are theme tokens now, and Reduce motion shows the card at once.

The brief and the Rules

  • The brief has at most one sentence: the lesson, during the first exams. Below it are the unwell row and chips. "Running late" comes first, then the exam's rules. A check means allowed ("Scratch paper", "ID: expired OK"), a cross means not allowed ("Nothing else", "Name: exact"), and a document means required ("2nd ID needed"). These come from the same rule profile the consequences use. The door keeps its rule lines until Phase 5.
  • The Rules screen starts with the same chips for the next exam. Then come seven sections of rows. Each row shows a name and one short line (≤ 8 words), and a tap opens the full explanation under it. Every explanation is still there, including the ones the tests protect: "Paper being visible is not a reason", that Remove being available "does not mean it is justified", and "not the truth of the room".
  • Built once, kept. The Rules used to be built again for every exam. They are now built once per theme and text size, and the next exam's chips change in place (ManualScreen.Refresh). On the A15 (a long career, the in-app frame probe), opening the Rules again takes 27 ms with no long frame, every time. The first open of a session still builds the page, in 467 ms; before, that build ran again for every exam (512 ms).
  • Short everywhere else too. The three intro cards are one sentence each (16 to 20 words a card, from 29 to 43). The save-damaged, dismissed and confirm cards are one sentence each. Career log lines are six words at most, and the support and ad toasts are a few words.

The guard

  • TextRuleTests reads every string literal in the player-facing sources: GameText, the screens and the router. Any literal over fourteen words fails, unless the allowlist names it with a reason. The allowlist has two groups. The Rules' details sit behind a row the player opens, and a separate check keeps each row's visible line short. The room's hints and the inspection and observation lines belong to Phase 5. A second check fails on any "¢" in Game or Core.
  • It also builds every event card in every wording and checks the template. It checks that no card body holds a number, that a known cost is on its answer, and that a hidden card shows no price. It checks that log lines, the intro, the brief chips and the cards around a career fit the limits.
  • Word budgets for whole screens: the hub ≤ 36 and each Shop or Wardrobe segment ≤ 120 (these already existed); Career ≤ 70, Settings ≤ 50, and the Rules ≤ 100 in the first view, ≤ 280 with every row closed and ≤ 65 per section.

Where the brief left room

  • Titles are one or two words, not always one. Two audits in a row must not look like one audit shown twice (M108), so an audit's title is its fault: "Expired ID", "Photo mismatch". A review's title is its finding: "Supported", "Against you", "No finding", "No credit".
  • Car trouble keeps its chip. Paying has a certain result. "Risk it" has an uncertain one, and the card says so in words ("or risk arriving late"). The witness card is treated as hidden, because admitting a mistake has a cost the card does not state.
  • A card of several witnesses has one line per witness, each a single sentence. Testimony is never cut to fit.
  • Two sentences keep their number: the crest award ("Earned in 6 exams at Riverside.", approved in Phase 3) and a tool-use reward, where the count is the whole sentence.
  • The reassignment and offer letters no longer list which exams the school sets, because the card already shows the school, its kind, its rating and its pay.
  • No localisation table exists. All text is English and lives in GameText, so no other language is out of date. Kept as before: the bribe's speaker stays "A parent", the witness title says what they "say", and a reaction never mentions coins or a later consequence.

The admission desk

  • The person beside their ID. "In person" is a portrait slot (character art drops in here later), the name and how they look. "Their ID" has one line per field, each with a glyph: a person for the name, a book for the exam, a building for the room, a clock for the time, a calendar for "valid until", an eye for the photo, and a document for a second ID when the exam asks for one. "This exam" below uses the same glyphs, so the book on the ID is matched against the book of the exam.

What to borrow, and what to do better

These draw on New Star Soccer (mobile), which the player named, and on current mobile game UI. Web sources say little about NSS's hub layout. Its 2016 v2 update gave the menus "a complete graphical facelift" that "streamlined navigation". The notes below are about patterns. No assets are copied.

New Star Soccer: borrow

  • One obvious next action. A big "next match" button anchors the career screen. Ours is "Go to next exam", but it floats under an empty area.
  • Relationships as bars, not sentences. NSS shows each relationship as a short labelled bar. We have reputation as "42 / 100" and probation as prose.
  • Money as a number you watch grow. Wages and bonuses are counted in and spent in a lifestyle shop.
  • Life events as short messages with two answers. Our event cards have the same shape; ours just say too much.

New Star Soccer: do better

  • Its menus are dated, by its own players' and reviewers' account: lists, small type and dense stat pages. Our base is already more polished.
  • No illustration. We have baked school and event art, so lead with it.
  • Feedback is mostly numbers changing. We can make money and standing physical: coins, stars and a stack you can see.

2026 mobile patterns worth using

  • A bottom tab bar as primary navigation, per Apple's HIG and Material 3, with 3–5 nouns and the active tab filled.
  • Hub of big tiles. Each tile has one icon, one word and one number or badge. Badges carry "new" or "affordable".
  • Bold numbers. A currency or score is the biggest type on its card, with tabular numerals and icons beside them.
  • Reward choreography: a burst, then a flight to the counter, which ticks per coin and pops once at the end. The source and the counter are on screen together.
  • Card stacks for decisions: one card at a time, illustration first, two big answers.
  • Icon chips instead of rule sentences, as in Clash Royale and Brawl Stars, with "ⓘ" for detail on demand.
