Performance on the Galaxy A15

Frame rate and freezes measured in the app on a Galaxy A15 5G (SM-S156V, Android 16, 1080 × 2340, 90 Hz panel, Mali-G57 on Vulkan). The probe splits every frame into main thread, render thread and GPU time. The judder was GPU load, not a frame-rate setting. The half-second freezes came from saving the career. All numbers are from development builds on that phone, before and after the fixes.

Why it juddered

14–15 ms

GPU time for an exam frame. A 90 Hz frame allows 11.1 ms, so every other frame missed its vsync: 11.1 / 22.2 ms.

17 ms

GPU time under a full-screen card. About 7 ms of that was the room being drawn behind a screen that hides it.

4–5 ms

Real main-thread work per frame. The CPU was never the limit.

  • The setting was already right. The target was 90 (M125), vSync was 0, and Optimized Frame Pacing was off. Turning on Swappy with a target of 60 didn't switch the panel to 60 Hz. It ran at 45.
  • Experiments on the same build (probe flag): HDR off made no change, and shadows off gave about 1 ms. Render scale 0.6 (from 0.8) brought an exam to 88.6 fps, but it softens the picture, and the cards still ran at 70.
  • Decision: a steady 45 on this phone. Every frame takes 22.2 ms, the same length, with no alternation, and half the frames means less battery and heat. A frame-rate governor starts at the panel's best even rate and steps down for the session only if more than 15% of ordinary frames miss. A phone that holds 90 keeps 90.

Why it froze

  • Every autosave cost 420–450 ms of main thread. The parts were: serialize the career (75–90 ms), read the temp file back and parse it (165–185 ms), then parse the old primary before rotating it (150–190 ms). Each save also allocated about 12 MB. Saves happen at every boundary: each decision at the door, twice at exam start, and at the result.
  • Not the suspects. Navmesh build: 4–5 ms. Room dressing: 5–8 ms. Spawning students: 13–60 ms. Shader compilation and resource loads: under 45 ms in any frame.
  • Fix: the career is serialized on the main thread and written on a worker, using the same write, verify and rotate order. A primary that the last save already verified isn't parsed again. Load, delete, pause and quit all wait for the worker.
  • What is left: serializing still takes 75–100 ms on the main thread. In this development build that leaves a 120–210 ms freeze per save.
